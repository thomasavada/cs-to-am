# Four workshops

After [00-live-talk.md](00-live-talk.md). **2 hours each.** Lecture is short; they do reps.  
**Joy stays out.** Graduation = they can say the merchant’s problem back in their own words.

Ticket into the room = last week’s homework on the table.

Textbook = **their real tickets**, not fake perfect English. Pull 30 subjects + 15 threads before session 1.

House example (do not change): **Lumi** — one moisturizer, $42, refill ~8 weeks. Follow one customer (Mai) from “saw it” to “bought a second time.”

Map, words, banned question: [02-shop-map.md](02-shop-map.md).

---

## Session 1 — The shop, so words have a place

**Leave with:** they can point at a website and name the **box**.

- Customer path once (Lumi): land → product → cart → pay. That path *is* the business.
- Admin, five items only: Products, Orders, Customers, Discounts, Analytics. Apps = extra employees.
- **Boxes drill:** throw a word, they slap it on a box.
- Pairs: 2 live Joy merchants, screenshot homepage → product → cart. Fill the card: what they sell, price, buy-again, when.

**Homework:** 10 real ticket subjects. Next to each: **one box**. Wrong box is fine. Blank is not.

---

## Session 2 — Why the owner is scared (basic ecom)

**Leave with:** they know *why* a sentence matters, not only which box.

Whiteboard, once:

> Lumi paid **$30** to get Mai. Mai bought **$42**. After product + Shopify + shipping, order 1 is thin. **If Mai never returns, the shop lost.** Order 2 is the business.

Four doors: ads / email / she remembers / you bribe with a discount.

Two fears only:

1. I keep buying new people who vanish.
2. I keep making this order cheaper for people who would have paid anyway.

**Drill:** same tickets. Now: **box + which fear + one line in Vietnamese** (the owner’s fear, not a translation of the English).

If they say “I don’t understand the English”: “Which box? Which fear?” They can answer that without fluent English.

**Homework:** 5 full tickets. Restate each in 2 Vietnamese sentences. No questions to the merchant.

---

## Session 3 — Merchant English as labels, not ESL

**Leave with:** a decoder. English is a sticker on a box they already know.

Not grammar. *This word always points here.* See word list on [02-shop-map.md](02-shop-map.md).

**Banned:** “Could you please provide more details?” / “Can you explain more?”

**Allowed:** one fork that proves they already understood 80%.

Example:

- Merchant: “Rewards aren’t working.”
- Wrong: “Could you explain what you mean?”
- Restate: “Members aren’t using the reward, so it’s not creating another order.”
- Fork: “Do they **not see** the balance, or they see it and **it’s not worth** using?”

**Homework:** 8 merchant emails. For each: restatement + at most one fork. Two questions = fail the item.

---

## Session 4 — Restate, then you may speak

**Leave with:** a messy English ticket becomes a restatement a teammate can act on. Still no Joy setup.

- Rules: restate first. Forward only with the restatement on top. “Please explain” = fail.
- Demo 3 ugly tickets (typos, long, mixed). Think out loud: box → fear → restatement → one fork or none.
- **12 tickets, timed**, 4 min each. Their queue, last 30 days.
- Live: one reads a ticket out loud, other restates without looking.

**Pass:** 8/12 restatements a lead would accept.  
**Not yet:** another stack of 12 next week. Not a lecture.

Forward template is on [02-shop-map.md](02-shop-map.md).

---

## Real-store lab (parallel, not a 5th lecture)

Not a development store. Tiny launched shop:

- one product
- real checkout (test payments or a cheap live product)
- one way a customer arrives (a link they send themselves is enough)
- one reason to come back

They must complete **order 1 → attempt order 2**. That is the simulation.
