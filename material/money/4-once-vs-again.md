# Bought once vs bought again

**Used in:** Session 1, beat 5 · pays off in Session 4
**See also:** `../cases/hexclad.md`

---


**One classification predicts most of what a merchant does.** Get this and merchant behaviour stops looking random.

| | **Bought once** | **Bought again and again** |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | mattress, cookware, luggage, furniture | soap, shampoo, razors, supplements, skincare, coffee |
| How many shots you get | **one** | many |
| So order 1 must | **be squeezed** — this is all you get | only cover itself; the money is later |
| What they do about it | bundles, upsells, free gift, warranty, accessories | thin first order on purpose, then retention |
| What you will find installed | bundle / upsell / AOV apps | subscription, email, **loyalty** |
| Growth comes from | referral, new products, new audiences | **repeat** |
| Do they need a loyalty program? | usually **no** — referral and range instead | usually **yes** |

**HexClad** is column one: expensive cookware, bought rarely. Their work goes into referral, community
and selling you more pans — not points.

**Soap, shampoo, razors** are column two. People are *already* buying these every month from somebody.
You do not have to create the demand. You only have to be the one they buy from — which is exactly
what a loyalty program is for.
