# AOV is not a metric, it is a weapon

**Used in:** Session 1, closing · Session 3

---


The part almost nobody explains, and the reason merchants chase AOV so hard.

Two shops sell the same thing to the same person, bidding in the same ad auction:

| | Shop A | Shop B |
|---|---|---|
| Average order | $60 | **$95** — bundles, upsells, a good threshold |
| Kept per order | ~$13 | ~$30 |
| Can afford to bid | up to $13 | **up to $30** |
| Who wins the auction | — | **Shop B, every time** |

Shop B does not have a better ad. It has a **fatter order**, so it can outbid Shop A for the same customer
forever. Shop A gets starved out of the auction it cannot afford to be in.

> **Raising AOV is how you buy the right to keep advertising.**

Same logic, one step further: a shop with **good retention** can afford to lose money on order 1 —
so it can outbid *everyone* who cannot. **Retention buys aggression.**

That is why "increase AOV" and "increase repeat rate" are not two separate merchant wishes.
They are the same wish: *let me pay more than my competitor for the same customer.*
