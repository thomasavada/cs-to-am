# The $30 shirt — order 1 vs order 2

**Used in:** Session 1, beat 3 (the whiteboard build)
**See also:** `3-allowable-cac.md`

---


Whiteboard. No slides. Do not move on until the room has argued.

> **"You can buy this shirt for $30. What do you sell it for?"**

Someone will say $60. Good. Now find out what they actually keep.

## Order 1

| | |
|---|---|
| Sticker price | $60.00 |
| 15% off, because a stranger needs a reason to try you | −$9.00 |
| **Collected** | **$51.00** |
| The shirt itself | −$30.00 |
| Shipping | −$6.00 |
| Payment processing (~2.9% + 30¢) | −$1.78 |
| Ads, to make this one person show up | −$15.00 |
| **Kept** | **−$1.78** |

**They lost money.** And they have not yet paid for: the Shopify plan, the apps, anybody's salary, rent, or tax.
The app stack alone is often **~10% of revenue** at this size — call it another $6.

So the real answer is closer to **−$8 on a $60 sale.**

Let that sit. Then ask the only question that matters:

> **So why would anybody run this business?**

## Order 2 — same customer, eight weeks later

| | |
|---|---|
| Sticker price | $60.00 |
| First-order discount | **none** — she is not a stranger now |
| The shirt | −$30.00 |
| Shipping | −$6.00 |
| Processing | −$2.04 |
| **Ads** | **$0.00 — she came back on her own** |
| **Kept** | **+$21.96** |

**Order 1 lost $2. Order 2 made $22.**

That is the business. Not the shirt. The **second** shirt.

And now the two owner fears are not slogans, they are these two tables:

1. *I keep buying new people who vanish* — a shop that only ever books the first table.
2. *I keep discounting people who would have paid anyway* — the $9, handed to someone who was already buying.
