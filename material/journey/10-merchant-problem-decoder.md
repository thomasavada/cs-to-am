# The merchant problem decoder

**Used in:** Session 1, after the funnel. **Source:** Chase Chappell, *"If You Watch One Facebook Ad Video"*
([42uhZYnyEXU](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=42uhZYnyEXU)), raw transcript in the Obsidian vault.

Filtered by the **outside-in rule**: the site and business half is here. The ad-account half
(CPM, compliance keywords, creative volume, CBO structure) and the TikTok Shop half are deliberately out.

A merchant never says *"I have a leak at the product page."* They say a number is bad, or that something
"isn't working." This is how to get from what they said to where the problem actually is.

---

## The table CS should be able to run

| They say / the number shows | What it actually means | Where on the journey | What fixes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sales flat, ROAS looks great** | Ads are re-reaching people who already buy. Meta claims credit for email sales. | acquisition ↔ come back | Exclude past customers + the email list from acquisition. Switch the objective to **first-time**. Offer first-time messaging on site *and* in creative. |
| **Low returning-customer rate** | Nothing brings them back — and the fix depends entirely on what they sell | come back | **consumable** → subscription (the big lever) + remarketing · **clothing** → retarget past buyers with 2nd–5th heroes, new arrivals, drops · **high-ticket one-time** → sell the consumable *attached* to it |
| **Low AOV** | Not enough money per order | cart / offer | 4-packs and 6-packs · pre-built pairs (moisturizer + exfoliator) · in-cart, on-site and popup upsells · cross-sells · **free-shipping threshold $1 above current AOV** · spend-$100-get-a-gift |
| **AOV under ~$35** | A hard floor | money | *"Anything below $35 is horrible and it's almost impossible to scale."* Fix AOV before anything else. |
| **Low ROAS, but good clicks, cheap reach, good CVR** | Nothing is broken. **AOV is the issue.** | money | Double AOV $35 → $70 and ROAS goes 1.2 → 2.4 **without touching the ads.** |
| **Good clicks, cheap reach, low CVR** | **A site problem, not an ads problem** | product page | See the PDP list below |
| **Low CVR but high AOV** | **Normal. Do not panic.** | — | A $1,700 machine will never convert like a $30 candle. It still works on the maths. Payment plans nudge it. |
| **~8% add to cart but ~1% conversion** | **90% of the time: shipping is too expensive.** Otherwise something is literally broken. | cart → checkout | See the checkout list below |

## When the product page is the problem

The concrete fixes, in the order given:

- **Three bullets** stating the offer clearly, right up top
- **Five to seven images** — ingredients, flavour, benefits, serving, lifestyle — so there are five to seven call-outs
- **Subscribe & save selected by default**
- **Remove the quantity selector** — you can add more from the cart anyway
- **Show the pack that is actually selected** — if it is a six-pack, show the six-pack
- **Banner offer + free shipping above the fold**, not buried at the bottom
- **Collapse the padding** so the first screen contains everything that matters

> The principle underneath: **move everything upward.** On first load she should see the offer, the proof
> and the price without scrolling.

## When something is broken at the cart or checkout

The named conversion killers — and **one of them is us**:

| Killer | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Shipping is too expensive** | $30 product, $20 to ship. 90% of cases. |
| A button covering the button | the add-to-cart or checkout CTA is partly hidden |
| **Cart page *and* drawer cart both loading** | double load, twice the wait — use the drawer, not a cart page |
| **A loyalty widget over the checkout button** | ← **this is our app** |
| A support widget over the CTA | same failure, different vendor |
| Too few payment options | three instead of fifteen — no Apple Pay, Google Pay, Klarna, Afterpay, Shop Pay |
| Shipping to a country they cannot actually ship to | targeting set wider than fulfilment |

### The one CS must never forget

> *"Maybe you have a loyalty button that's 10% off if you claim it, but your checkout button's here,
> and a support button there, and the only spot you can click is this little spot.
> **That's a conversion killer.**"*

**Our widget can be the reason a merchant's checkout is failing.** Said out loud in the room, this is the
moment "placement" stops being a cosmetic preference and becomes something that costs the merchant money.

It also reframes an annoying ticket. When a merchant says *"can you move the widget?"* — that is not
fussiness. They may have watched their conversion rate fall. **Move it, and ask what changed.**

---

## How to use this in the room

Do not hand it out as a list to memorise — that is exactly the failure this course exists to fix.
Use it as the **answer key** while pairs work a real brand:

1. They name a symptom on the brand they are reading
2. They say where on the journey it lives
3. *Then* check it here

The goal is that in a year they can rebuild most of this table themselves, because they understand the
path — not because they remembered a page.
