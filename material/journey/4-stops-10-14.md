# Stops 10–14 — the wait to the second order

**Used in:** Session 3, beats 2–6

---


**Mai does:** nothing. Waits. Then starts wondering.

**The owner is doing:** managing anxiety. The gap between *paid* and *arrived* is where every
**"where is my order?"** ticket is born — and WISMO is usually the single largest ticket category in ecom.

Shipping notification, tracking link, honest delay message. Boring, and it decides whether she buys again.

**Usually runs on:** Klaviyo, Wonderment, Gorgias
**Breaks when:** it silently ships late. A late parcel someone warned you about is fine. A late parcel
nobody mentioned is a refund request and a one-star review.
**Box:** AFTER THE ORDER

---

## Stop 11 — The emails after

**Mai gets, over the next weeks:**

| When | Email | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Immediately | order confirmation | receipt, reassurance |
| Ships | tracking | kill the WISMO ticket |
| Delivered | "how to use it" | **make sure she actually uses it** |
| ~Week 2 | review request | proof for the next stranger |
| ~Week 6 | "running low?" | **the money email** |

**The owner is doing:** the thing that separates a shop from a store. Note the review request is timed to
when she has *used* it — not when it arrived. A review on day one is a review of the packaging.

**And week 6 is the one that pays.** The refill window is ~8 weeks, so you arrive *before* she runs out
and before she thinks about alternatives. **This email is worth more than the ad from stop 1.**

**Box:** AFTER THE ORDER → COME BACK

## Stop 12 — Getting her back, everywhere at once

Mai now exists in more than one place, and the modern shop is hunting her across all of them:

- **Retargeting** — because Lumi has her email, they can match her on Meta or TikTok. Far cheaper than
  stop 1, because they are no longer paying to find a stranger; they are paying to remind a customer.
- **Creators / TikTok** — an army of small creators posting about it. Some sell in TikTok Shop directly,
  with its own checkout that never touches Shopify.
- **Amazon** — plenty of people discover on TikTok and then buy on Amazon out of habit.

**This is why attribution is a swamp.** Mai saw the ad, got the email, watched a creator, bought on Amazon.
Which one gets the credit? CS does not have to solve this — but they must **stop being surprised by it**
when a merchant says their numbers do not match.

**Box:** GET PEOPLE IN + COME BACK

## Stop 13 — Referral

**Mai does:** likes it enough to tell someone.

**The owner is doing:** turning a happy customer into the cheapest acquisition there is. A referred person
arrives with **trust pre-loaded**, converts better, and stays longer — and it is paid only on success,
unlike stop 1, which is paid on hope.

**This is a different machine from loyalty.** Referral **grows** the base. Loyalty **monetises** it.
**Box:** GET PEOPLE IN

## Stop 14 — The second order, and where Joy finally lives

**Mai does:** week 8, nearly out. Gets the refill email. Opens the account page and sees **420 points —
worth $4.20 off.** Reorders.

**The owner is doing:** the only thing that ever made this business work.

**Notice where we are: stop 14 of 14.** Loyalty is not the engine of a shop. It is the **last mile of a
long system**, and it only pays if the thirteen stops before it exist. That is the whole reason Joy is
the last file in this folder and not the first.

And notice what the points actually did: they did not make Mai like Lumi. They gave her a **reason to
choose Lumi instead of the alternative in the same moment** — and a small nudge to do it **now** rather than
in three weeks. That is what loyalty buys: **not affection. Timing and preference.**

**Box:** COME BACK → MONEY
