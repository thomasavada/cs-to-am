# Stops 1–9 — ad to thank-you page

**Used in:** Session 2, beats 4–8

---


**Mai sees:** a 20-second phone video. A woman with the same skin problem Mai has, saying it out loud in the
first two seconds. Then the jar. Then a before/after. Then "3,000 five-star reviews."

**Why it works — the shape underneath:** hook (name her problem) → proof (a real face, not a model) →
offer (why now). This is what "understand the content, understand the story" means: an ad is not a picture
of a product, it is **an argument aimed at one person**.

**The owner is doing:** paying for attention. Every scroll costs money whether she clicks or not.

**Costs:** ~$30 of ad spend before one Mai actually arrives.
**Breaks when:** the ad promises something the landing page does not repeat.
**Box:** GET PEOPLE IN

---

## Stop 2 — The click, and where she lands

**Mai sees:** either the homepage (and has to hunt) or the exact product from the video.

**The owner is doing:** trying not to waste the $30 they just spent. The single most common leak in ecom is
**ad says one thing, page says another** — different photo, different claim, different price.

**Breaks when:** the ad shows a promotion the page never mentions. She assumes she misread it and leaves.
**Box:** ON THE SITE

---

## Stop 3 — The popup

**Mai sees:** after ~5 seconds or one scroll — *"10% off your first order."* She gives her email. Maybe SMS too.

**The owner is doing:** buying her contact details. This is the real transaction here — **not the sale, the email.**
Without it, everything from stop 7 onward is impossible. Lumi cannot email a stranger.

**Costs:** the 10% is real. And here is the uncomfortable part — **some people who take that 10% would have
paid full price.** That is owner **fear #2**, live, in the first thirty seconds.

**Usually runs on:** Klaviyo, Attentive, Postscript
**Breaks when:** it fires instantly, on mobile, before she has seen anything. She bounces.
**Box:** ON THE SITE (getting the email)

---

## Stop 4 — Browsing, and the product page

**Mai sees:** photos, ingredients, **reviews with real faces**, a shipping promise, maybe "only 8 left."

**The owner is doing:** answering the three silent questions — *will this work for me? can I trust you?
what happens if I hate it?* Reviews answer all three cheaper than any copy.

**Usually runs on:** Okendo, Judge.me, Stamped, Loox
**Breaks when:** no reviews, or a returns policy that is hidden. A stranger will not risk $42 on a shop
that will not say what happens if it fails.
**Box:** ON THE SITE

---

## Stop 5 — Subscribe or bundle

**Mai sees:** two radio buttons. *One-time $42* / *Subscribe & save $35.70.* Or: *buy 2, save 15%.*

**The owner is doing:** two very different things, and CS must not confuse them:

| | What it buys the owner | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Subscription** | **LTV** — the next order is already agreed | 15% margin, forever |
| **Bundle** | **AOV** — a fatter order today | less margin per unit, but more cash now |

**Subscription raises lifetime value. Bundle raises order value.** They solve different problems.
A shop with a repeat problem needs the first. A shop with a thin-order problem needs the second.

**Usually runs on:** Recharge, Appstle, Skio (sub) · Rebuy (bundle)
**Breaks when:** the sub discount is so deep the shop loses money on loyal customers — the exact people
who would have re-ordered anyway. **Fear #2 again.**
**Box:** PAY / COME BACK

---

## Stop 6 — The cart

**Mai sees:** a drawer slides out. *"You're $12 away from free shipping."* Underneath: *"Add the travel size?"*

**The owner is doing:** using the strongest lever in ecom. **Free shipping thresholds move AOV more than
almost anything else**, because Mai would rather add $12 of product than pay $7 of shipping for nothing.

**Costs:** the shipping the shop now eats. The threshold has to be set above the point where that maths works.
**Usually runs on:** Rebuy, native Shopify
**Breaks when:** the threshold is so far above her basket it reads as a wall instead of a nudge.
**Box:** PAY

---

## Stop 7 — She leaves

**Mai does:** puts the phone down. Dinner. She was never angry — she just left.

**This is normal.** Most carts are abandoned. It is not a failure, it is the default.

**The owner is doing:** three different recoveries for three different exits:

| She left from | Email that fires | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| A product page | browse abandon | "still thinking about this?" |
| The cart | cart recovery | "you left this — here it is" |
| Checkout | checkout recovery | strongest intent, chase hardest |

**And this is the whole reason stop 3 existed.** No email captured → no recovery → the $30 is simply gone.
That causal chain is the single clearest example of why a shop is a **system**, not a pile of features.

**Usually runs on:** Klaviyo, Attentive
**Box:** ON THE SITE → PAY

---

## Stop 8 — Checkout

**Mai sees:** Shop Pay / Apple Pay at the top. Trust badges. Card logos. Shipping options. A discount code field.
Then, after paying, a one-click offer.

**The owner is doing:** removing every remaining reason to stop. Checkout is where intent goes to die:

- **Shipping cost shock is the number one abandon reason.** A $7 fee on a $42 order reads as a 17% price rise.
- **The discount code box is a leak.** It sends people off to hunt for a code — some never return.
- **Express wallets** cut a five-field form to one thumbprint.
- **Guest checkout.** Forcing account creation on a first-time stranger loses orders.

**On Shopify Plus** this is where the difference shows: checkout can be customised, extensions can add
upsells and custom fields, scripts can run tiered logic. On standard Shopify, checkout is mostly fixed.

**The numbers CS hears about live here:** add-to-cart rate, checkout rate, conversion rate.
They are not metrics to optimise. They are **doors, and this is where people stop walking through.**

**Box:** PAY

---

## Stop 9 — The thank-you page

**Mai sees:** "Order confirmed." Then: *"Add the night cream for $18 — one click, no card needed."*

**The owner is doing:** working the **highest-intent real estate in the entire shop.** She has already
decided to trust them and her card is already charged. Post-purchase upsell has no risk of losing the sale —
the sale is done.

**This is also where loyalty enrolment belongs**, and where most shops waste it.
**Box:** PAY → AFTER THE ORDER
