# CS vs AM — what actually changes

The destination of this whole folder. Read it before `2-course.md` makes sense.

**The one line:**

> CS is judged on **the ticket**. AM is judged on **the shop**.

The trap sentence — say it in the room:

> You can close every ticket perfectly and still lose the account.

That is not a criticism of CS. It is the reason the job is different. Nothing in the CS scoreboard
tells you the merchant set up Joy, issued 40,000 points, and nobody ever redeemed one.

VN, for the room:

> CS đóng ticket. AM làm cho shop có khách quay lại. Đóng hết ticket mà shop vẫn chết thì mình vẫn thua.

## The table

| | **CS** | **AM** |
|---|---|---|
| What you are actually doing | **execution** | **outcome** |
| Unit of work | one **ticket** | one **account**, over time |
| Who starts | they write to you | **you** open the conversation |
| Who you talk to | whoever wrote in | the person who **decides** |
| Time horizon | today, this reply | the quarter, the renewal |
| What you must know | **the app** | **the shop** |
| Question you answer | "where do I click?" | "is this making you money?" |
| What *done* means | ticket closed | the **outcome showed up** |
| When you are wrong | backlog, slow reply, bad CSAT | churn you did not see coming |
| To the company | cost to serve | **carries revenue** — renewal + expansion |
| Judgement required | is this the right **setting** | is this the right **merchant**, right **move**, right **now** |
| Your instrument | decoder + boxes → `7-shop-map.md` | teardown sheet → `6-teardown.md` |
| Your fear | "I don't understand the English" | "this account is quietly dying and I'll find out at renewal" |

## Why now — the execution moved to machines

The two columns above are not just two jobs. They are **execution** and **outcome**, and only one of
them is still scarce.

| | Can a machine do it? |
|---|---|
| "Where do I turn on points?" | **Yes.** Instantly, 24/7, in perfect English, in every language. |
| Write a clear, polite, correct reply | **Yes.** Better than any of us. |
| Read the docs and configure the rule | **Yes.** |
| Decide whether points are the right move for *this* merchant | **No.** |
| Own whether their customers actually came back | **No.** |
| Be accountable across a quarter to a person who trusts you | **No.** |

**AI does not take the job. It takes the execution.** What is left for people is the outcome —
judgement about a specific business, and accountability over time.

So "CS → AM" is not a promotion path any more. It is where the work went.

**And it kills the English excuse twice.** Not only is English the wrong diagnosis
(the real gap is no picture of the shop) — it is also the **worst possible thing to compete on.**
Perfect English is the single easiest thing a machine produces. A picture of this merchant's shop is not.

> If your plan is to win on English, you picked the one race that is already lost.

## The scoreboards

Look at these side by side. They barely overlap — that is the whole point.

| CS is measured on | AM is measured on |
|---|---|
| Tickets closed | Did they **renew** |
| First response time | Did they **expand** (Advanced → Ultimate) |
| CSAT on the reply | Did the program **actually launch** |
| Backlog size | **Time to first result** |
| — | Are the merchant's customers **coming back** |

Every CS number can be green while every AM number is red. That gap is the job.

## Same ticket, two answers

This is the comparison that teaches. Everything above is theory until you see it on one message.

### 1. "How do I make points 2x on this product?"

**CS:** Settings → Earning → select product → multiplier → Save. *Answered. Closed.*

**AM:** *Why that product?* Two normal reasons, opposite treatments:
- **Slow inventory** they want to move → points are the wrong tool, a discount or bundle is faster
- **Hero product** they want people to re-buy → then tie it to the refill window, not just the multiplier

> "Before I set this — is that your best seller you want people re-ordering, or stock you're trying to clear?
> The setup is different."

### 2. "Rewards aren't working."

**CS:** "Could you please provide more details?" → merchant writes the ticket properly → copy → forward.

**AM:** restate, then **one** fork:
- Restate: "Members aren't using the reward, so it isn't creating a second order."
- Fork: "Do they **not see** the balance, or they see it and **it's not worth** using?"

Both answers take the same number of minutes. Only one of them did the thinking.

### 3. "We want to cancel."

**CS:** processes it. Maybe offers a discount to stay.

**AM:** this is never a pricing event, it is a **result** event.
- What did they expect when they launched? More repeat orders.
- What happened? Points issued, nothing redeemed → the program never reached order 2.
- The discount does not fix that. The redemption path does.

### 4. "Can we email someone when they reach VIP?"

**CS:** that's on Ultimate / here is the setting.

**AM:** reads the signal — they are trying to make the tier **mean something**.
This merchant is past setup and into outcome. That is an expansion conversation *and* a healthy account,
and it should be logged as both.

## What AM is NOT

Guardrails. Each of these has already killed a version of this course.

| Not | Because |
|---|---|
| **Senior CS** | Not harder tickets, faster. A **different unit of work.** This is the most common misread. |
| A media buyer | We never run their ads. We only need to know traffic **costs money**. |
| A business consultant | We do not rewrite their strategy. We make **our** part produce a result. |
| A sales rep | Expansion is a consequence of the outcome, not the goal you chase. |
| Someone who stopped doing tickets | AMs read tickets. They just read them as **signals about an account**. |

## What transfers, what has to be added

**Already have it — keep it:**
- Patience, tone, writing to a stranger who is annoyed
- Knowing what Joy can and cannot do
- Speed

**Missing — this is the entire course:**
- A picture of a shop, so merchant sentences land somewhere (`02`)
- Money on one unit — why order 2 is the business (rep 1)
- Reading a brand cold: how they get people, where people quit, why anyone returns (reps 2–3)
- Reading a stack and judging fit (rep 4)
- Having built a real store, so you know what those choices cost (build track)
- Restating before speaking (the gate)

**Does not transfer at all:** knowing where the buttons are. That is the thing they currently have most of.

---

## The ladder — there is a step in between

You do not go CS → AM. There are three rungs, and each has a gate.

```
CS                  answers the app
  ↓  gate: restate the merchant's outcome before you reply. 8/12.
CS who reads         answers the ticket behind the ticket
  ↓  gate: cold brand teardown a lead accepts, in 15 min. Own store launched.
AM                   answers for the account
```

The middle rung is a real job and a real raise. Most of this cohort should be aiming there first.
Not everyone will want rung 3 — owning revenue and renewals is a different appetite, not a better person.

---

## Where the course lands them

`2-course.md` finishes at the **top of rung 2, standing at the gate to rung 3**.

After four reps they can look at a brand they have never seen and say what it sells, how it makes money,
how people arrive, why anyone comes back, what it runs, and whether it is ours — without opening Joy.

That is the AM read. What is still missing after the course is the AM **job**: owning a portfolio,
prioritising which accounts get your week, running the renewal conversation, carrying a number.
That is the file after the Joy last-mile file. Do not start either yet.
