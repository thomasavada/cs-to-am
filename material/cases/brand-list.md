## Verified detail

### Halfdays — `halfdays.com`  ·  *session 1*
Shopify · **Klaviyo** · Yotpo **Reviews** · Rebuy · **no loyalty**
Free shipping **$95+** · outdoor apparel, women's ski/outerwear

**Teaches:** the shirt drill, on a real product. Apparel has a *seasonal* repeat reason — a new drop, a
second layer — not a refill one, so "would anyone buy twice?" has an interesting answer.
Rebuy installed means they know their orders are too thin. Ask why the threshold is $95.

### Crown Affair — `crownaffair.com`  ·  *session 2, strong repeat*
Shopify · **Klaviyo · Attentive · Postscript · Recharge · Okendo · Gorgias** · **no loyalty**
Free shipping **$75+** · haircare — oil, shampoo, tools

**Teaches:** the fullest owned-channel stack you will see. Email **and** SMS **and** WhatsApp-class
messaging, plus subscription. Every "way back" from session 2 is installed here except one — ours.
A near-perfect ICP that also works as the retention example.

### HexClad — `hexclad.com`  ·  *session 2 weak repeat + session 3 non-fit*
Shopify · Klaviyo · Postscript · Okendo · Rebuy · **Rivo loyalty — installed and live**
Hero pans **$100–$129+** · free shipping always · `loyalty-program`, `loyalty-opt-in`, `Referral`, `Rewards` all in source

**Teaches — and this is better than a clean non-fit:** they run a loyalty program on a product
bought once every five years. So do not say *"HexClad shouldn't run loyalty."* Say:

> **They do run it. Go and look at what it actually rewards.**

Then let the room find that it is built around **referral and buying the next thing** — a lid, a knife,
another size — not around re-buying the same pan next month. Which is exactly the high-ticket answer.
The brand proves the lesson instead of being a strawman for it.

For **session 3** it fails the ICP checklist on the most visible line there is: a competitor is already
installed. That is a real "not ours", found in sixty seconds, with no opinion required.

### Rae Wellness — `raewellness.co`  ·  *session 3, the money shot*
Shopify · **Klaviyo · Recharge** · Yotpo **Reviews** · **no loyalty** · `/pages/rewards` → **404**
Products **$19.99–$30** · free shipping **$30+**

**Teaches:** the pattern in Joy's own ICP, standing still — subscription revenue, a way to reach people,
and nothing that gives a reason to *choose* them. Do it live, thirty seconds, ending on the 404.

## The trap to teach with this list

Rae Wellness and Halfdays both return a hit for **`yotpo`**. Neither has a loyalty program.

| What you find | What it is |
|---|---|
| `yotpo-product-reviews` | Yotpo **Reviews** — not loyalty |
| `swell`, `loyalty-program`, `loyalty-opt-in` | Yotpo **Loyalty** / Rivo / Smile — a real competitor |

> **A stack hit is a signal, not a verdict.** Yotpo sells reviews, SMS *and* loyalty as separate products.
> Confirm which one before you tell anybody a brand "already has loyalty."

That distinction is worth a slide of its own — it is the difference between a careful read and a guess,
and it is the kind of mistake that loses credibility on a call.

## Free-shipping thresholds, side by side

A live illustration of the *$1 above current AOV* rule from session 1:

| Brand | Threshold | Typical product |
|---|---|---|
| Rae Wellness | **$30** | $19.99–$30 |
| Crown Affair | **$75** | oil/shampoo, mid-price |
| Halfdays | **$95** | outerwear |
| HexClad | always free | $100+ pans |

Ask the room: *why is Rae's $30 and Halfdays' $95?* The answer is each sits just above where that shop's
average order already lands — which is the rule, visible in the wild, on four sites, in two minutes.
