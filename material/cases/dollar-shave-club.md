# Dollar Shave Club — how you shock a price

**Used in:** Session 1, beat 6 (watch the video)
**See also:** `../loyalty/4-referral.md`

---


A retail razor cartridge is expensive, and **most of what you pay is not razor.** It is the layers
from section 1, plus a shelf in a supermarket, plus decades of advertising.

Dollar Shave Club did not invent a better blade. They **deleted the layers**:

| | Retail razor | Dollar Shave Club |
|---|---|---|
| Route to you | factory → brand → distributor → supermarket | factory → **you** |
| Discovery | TV, shelf position | one viral video, then **referral** |
| Purchase | you remember to go buy one | **subscription** — the next one is already agreed |
| Price | high, because of the layers | a fraction of it |
| Margin | split across everyone | **kept** |

> **Their innovation was the price structure, not the product.**

And notice what replaced each deleted layer: **subscription** replaced "remembering to buy,"
**referral** replaced the shelf, and going direct replaced the distributor. That is why those two things
exist in their business — not because someone liked the features.

**This is the case study to teach with,** because it shows a whole business model as an answer to a
pricing problem.
