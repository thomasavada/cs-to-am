# HexClad — the brand that should not run points

**Used in:** Session 1 (bought-once), Session 4 (stage). **The uncomfortable case.**

---

Premium hybrid cookware. Expensive. Bought **rarely** — a pan is a five-year decision, not an eight-week one.

## Why it is in the course

It is the clearest column-one brand in `../money/4-once-vs-again.md`:

| | HexClad |
|---|---|
| Bought | **once**, maybe twice in a decade |
| So order 1 must | **be squeezed** — this may be all they ever get |
| What they do | bundles and sets, upsells, accessories, warranty, community |
| Growth comes from | **referral**, new products, new audiences |
| Does a points program make sense? | **Mostly no** |

## The lesson

A points program needs a repurchase cycle to run on. Give someone 400 points on a $300 pan they will not
replace for five years, and the points expire before they are ever worth anything. You have added a widget,
a cost and a promise, and produced no second order.

That does **not** make HexClad a bad brand or a small one. It makes it a brand whose repeat problem has a
different answer — sell them the *next* thing (a lid, a knife, a pan in another size), or get them to
bring someone new.

> **Some very good brands should not run a points program.**
> If CS cannot say that out loud, they are selling, not advising.

## How to use it in the room

Pair it against Lumi or any refill brand. Same question to both — *why would a human buy this twice?* —
and let the room hear how different the two honest answers are.

Then ask the real one: **if HexClad wrote to Joy tomorrow, what would you tell them?**
(Referral. Range expansion. Not points. And say why.)
