# Does this shop need a loyalty program at all?

**Used in:** Session 4, beat 6 · full version in Block 2
**See also:** `../money/4-once-vs-again.md`

---


**A loyalty program is a multiplier on a base you already have. It is not a growth engine.**

Say it with numbers on the board:

| | Shop A | Shop B |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | 100 | 20,000 |
| Loyal share | **100%** | **5%** (1,000 people) |
| Extra orders from loyalty | ~100 | ~3,000–4,000 |
| Verdict | **still dead** | real money |

Shop A has a perfect loyalty program and is going out of business. You cannot multiply your way out of
a base of 100. **Multiply a small number, it is still small.**

So the first question is never "which features." It is **what stage are they at.**

| Their stage | What they actually need |
|---|---|
| Running ads, trying to survive, no real base yet | **Expand the base.** Acquisition, referral. **Not a points program.** |
| Base exists, but people buy once and vanish | A **reason to return** → points, tiers |
| Base exists, repeat is okay, but they discount everyone | **Stop the blanket discount** → VIP tiers |
| Base exists and already subscribes | Loyalty **on top** of subscription — different job (see 5.4) |

Telling a survival-stage shop to launch points is not service. It is selling them the wrong thing,
and it will not produce a result, and they will churn — correctly.

**Drill:** three real brands from the earlier reps. For each: what stage, and do they need this at all?
"No, not yet" must be an allowed answer, or the drill is fake.
