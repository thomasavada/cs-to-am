# Points vs store credit

**Used in:** Block 2

---


> **Points are the brand's currency. Store credit is just money with your logo on it.**

| | **Points** | **Store credit** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | brand currency — abstract | **money**, denominated in $ |
| Customer understands it | slowly, needs explaining | instantly |
| How it feels | belonging, progress, a game | a transaction, cash back |
| Brand attachment | **strong** — you name it, you theme it | weak — it is just money |
| Can reward non-purchase? | yes — review, signup, birthday, referral | awkward |
| Redemption rate | lower (that is partly the point) | **high** — it is cash, people spend it |
| Cost to the merchant | some issued points are never used | **you are holding their money** |
| Best at | building a habit, tiers, long repurchase cycles | returns and refunds, keeping money in the shop |

Both are real answers. **Neither is the default.**

- Merchant sells a **considered, expensive, rare** purchase → store credit is clearer, points feel silly
- Merchant sells a **refill / consumable / frequent** item → points build the habit, tiers give a ladder
- Merchant does a lot of **returns** → store credit keeps the money in the shop instead of refunding out
- Merchant wants customers to **feel like members** → points, named, on-brand

The trade-off in one line: store credit is understood immediately and used immediately — that is its
strength *and* its cost. Points are less liquid and more emotional.

**Drill:** merchant says "I want store credit." Do not answer. Ask stage, product, repurchase cycle,
return rate. Then recommend — and be able to say why the other one loses.
