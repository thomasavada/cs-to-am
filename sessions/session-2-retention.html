06
SMS
Read in minutes.
Roughly a hundred times more expensive per message than email — and read by almost everyone, almost immediately.
So use it for
  • a drop going live
  • a sale ending tonight
  • back in stock
  • an order problem
Never for a newsletter.
Overuse and she does not just ignore it — she unsubscribes, and you have lost the most powerful channel you had, permanently. A merchant asking “can I text everyone weekly?” is about to burn an asset.
07
WhatsApp
For most of the world,
this is the phone.
Vietnam, India, Brazil, Indonesia, most of Europe, most of Latin America. Not the US — which is why US-centric advice keeps missing it.
What makes it different
Support and selling happen in the same thread. She can just reply. It reads like a person, not a broadcast.
What it demands
Explicit opt-in, and pre-approved templates for anything you send first. You cannot just blast.
If a merchant sells into these markets and is only doing email, they are missing the channel their customers actually live in.
08
Retargeting
Not paying to find her.
Paying to remind her. She is already matched — from the pixel, or from the email list uploaded as an audience.
Why it is cheaper
Prospecting pays to reach strangers, most of whom will never care. Retargeting reaches people who already raised a hand.
Same ad money, far better odds.
But it is shrinking.
Privacy changes mean fewer people can be matched at all. Every year, a bigger share of “come back” has to happen on channels you own — which is the whole argument for email, SMS, and an account she logs into.
09
Organic social & content
Rented reach.
Free in money. Expensive in time. And you do not control who sees it — you built the audience, the algorithm decides.
What it is good at
Staying in her head between purchases, so that when the need comes back, you are the name she already knows.
What it cannot do
Reach a specific customer at a specific moment. You cannot post at one person who is about to run out.
Which is exactly the gap email, SMS and loyalty fill. Social makes her remember you. Owned channels make her buy.
10
Deals and sales — the fastest way, and the most dangerous
It works immediately
Nothing brings people back faster than money off. BFCM, a seasonal sale, a winback code — the orders arrive.
And then it keeps working
Which is the problem. Do it every November and people learn to wait for November. You did not discover demand. You moved it — and taught them the real price is lower.
This is fear #2, at the scale of a whole calendar.
A merchant whose only way back is a sale has not built retention. They have built a habit of waiting. That is the conversation to have with them — and it is the one nobody else is having.

05
A trap you will meet in a real account
Sales are flat.
But ROAS looks great.
What is actually happening
The ads are being shown to people who already buy. They would have bought anyway. Meta counts the sale, ROAS looks wonderful, and the business does not grow a dollar.
The number that reveals it
80%
returning customers
Healthy on its own. Fatal if it is also who the ads are hitting.
A high returning rate is a good thing. It becomes a problem only when acquisition stops reaching anyone new.
The fix is not our product — it is excluding past customers and the email list from acquisition. But spotting it is exactly the AM read, and almost nobody spots it.
12a
Low returning rate — the fix depends entirely on what they sell
Consumable
soap, supplements, skincare
Subscription is the biggest lever, then reminders and remarketing. This is where loyalty works hardest.
Clothing & repeat-catalogue
Do not just push one hero SKU. Show past buyers the 2nd to 5th best sellers, new arrivals, the next drop. Loyalty and credit help here, but range does more.
High-ticket, bought once
Sell them a consumable accessory — the cleaning kit, the refill, the spare. That is how a one-time product gets a second order at all.
So “they do not come back” is three different problems with three different answers.
And only one of them is mainly ours.

09
Two machines, opposite jobs
Referral
grows the base
New people arrive with trust already loaded. And you pay only when it works — unlike an ad, which you pay on hope.
Loyalty
monetises the base
The same people, chosen again. Nothing new arrives.
Remember this. Next session it decides what you recommend to a real merchant.
10
They ask for store credit. Do you switch it on?
Why
Credit fits — considered, rare, returns
Expensive one-off purchases, or a shop with lots of returns. Credit is understood instantly and keeps the money in the shop instead of refunding it out.
Why not
Points fit — refill, habit, membership
Credit is money with your logo on it — transactional, no attachment, and you are holding their cash. Points are the brand’s currency, and they build a habit.
11
Points
The brand’s currency
  • abstract — needs explaining
  • feels like belonging, progress
  • you name it, you theme it
  • some are never redeemed
Store credit
Money with your logo on it
  • understood instantly
  • feels like a transaction
  • looks like money, not like you
  • spent fast — you hold their cash
Which is why on-brand is not decoration.
If the widget looks like a generic app bolted on, it is not brand currency any more — it is a coupon machine, and you threw away the only reason you chose points.

13
Where the number comes from
  • Pull customers with total spend, last 12 months
  • Sort, highest first
  • Decide the share per tier — commonly ~5% top, ~20% middle
  • The spend at that cut line is your threshold
Too high
nobody reaches it — decoration
Too low
everybody clears it — a discount for everyone. Fear #2 with extra steps.
If you cannot say why the number is that number, do not set it.
And the reason tiers exist at all is fear two: otherwise you hand the same coupon to a first-time buyer and to someone who spends $2,000 a year.
