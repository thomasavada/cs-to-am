But does this shop need a loyalty program at all?

A loyalty program is a multiplier

SHOP A — 100 customers, 100% loyal

≈100 extra orders — still dead

SHOP B — 20,000 customers, 5% loyal

≈3,000–4,000 extra orders — real money

Multiply a small number — it is still small.

A shop with 100 customers wants points. Do you sell it?

WHY — no — they need a bigger base

Perfect retention on 100 people is still 100 people. They need referral and acquisition first. Points multiply a base that is not there yet.

WHY NOT — and if you sell it anyway

It will not produce a result. They will churn in six months — correctly — and blame us. You did not win an account, you borrowed one.

So the first question is stage

no real base yet
→
expand the base — REFERRAL
a base, but they buy once
→
a reason to return — POINTS
a base, discounting everyone
→
stop the blanket discount — TIERS

Referral grows the base. Loyalty monetises it. Never confuse the two.

The hardest thing you will have to say

They installed a bundle app. Their real leak is that nobody comes back.

They are fixing the cart while bleeding at the second order.

Do you tell them?

The conversation, replaced

Never: "Points or store credit? OK, I’ll show you where to turn it on."

  • 1 Stage — is there a base to sell back to?
  • 2 Base — how many, how many return, typical basket
  • 3 Fear — losing new people, or over-discounting?
  • 4 Mechanism — referral, points, credit, tiers
  • 5 Numbers — thresholds from their data, defended
  • 6 Placement — from their journey, not the demo store

Only step 6 is a screen.
