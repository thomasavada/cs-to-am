Why now · 1
The machine already answers
“where do I click?”
What it does better than us
  • instantly, 24/7, every language
  • perfect, polite English
  • reads the docs, writes the reply
  • never tired, never annoyed
What it cannot do
  • decide if points suit this merchant
  • own whether her customers came back
  • be accountable across a quarter
AI does not take the job. It takes the execution.
Which means the half of the job that is left for people is the outcome. Notice too which half the machine is best at: the English. If your plan is to win on English, you picked the one race already lost.
05
Why now · 2
Every loyalty app has
the same features.
Rivo · Yotpo · Smile · LoyaltyLion · Growave
Points. Tiers. Referrals. Widgets. All of them. Any feature we ship, they ship within a quarter.
So the app cannot be what a merchant chooses us for.
What is left to compete on
Whether the merchant actually gets a result — and how fast.
We are not selling software. We are selling the outcome the software produces.
Which means the people who can produce that outcome are not a support cost. They are the product.
06
And what it does for you on Monday
This is not homework for a promotion later.
  • You stop being stuck on tickets you do not understand
  • You stop asking her to explain — you already know what she means
  • Your forwards get actioned, because “I think…” is filled in
  • Fewer angry follow-ups, because you solved the real thing
  • Faster replies, because you are not decoding word by word
  • The copy-forward loop — the part that makes the job feel bad — stops
So today we do not open Joy. We start with the business.

Two laws to remember
The further a product travels from the factory,
the more mouths it has to feed.
A discount does not eat the profit.
It eats the layer paying for everything else.
Do the arithmetic on that second one
adidas keeps $2 on a $100 shoe.
A 10% discount is $10.
That does not shrink the profit. It erases it five times over.
Every time a merchant flinches at a discount, this is the number in their head.

So why would anybody
run this business?
Answers you will hear
“Sell more of them.” — volume does not help; every unit loses the same.
“Raise the price.” — fewer people buy, and the ad costs more per sale.
“Spend less on ads.” — then nobody arrives at all.
The only answer that works
Sell to the same person again, when finding her costs nothing.

21
Not every product is the same business
Bought once
mattress · cookware · luggage
One shot. So squeeze order 1 — bundles, upsells, warranty, a free gift.
Growth comes from new people: referral, new products.
Bought again
soap · shampoo · razors · skincare
Many shots. Order 1 only has to cover itself.
The money is in orders 2, 3 and 4.
One question — bought once, or bought again? — predicts most of what a merchant does.
Including whether they need us at all. Session four is built on this.

Then predict the stack
Bought once → expect bundles and upsells.
Bought again → expect subscription and email.
Say it out loud before you look at the source.
Then look. Were you right?
This is the whole skill in one exercise: read the product, predict the business, check yourself. Being roughly right, fast, beats being precisely right, slowly.
