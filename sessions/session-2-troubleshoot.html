06
When the product page is the problem
Move everything upward.
On first load she should see the offer, the proof and the price — without scrolling once.
  • Three bullets stating the offer, up top
  • Five to seven images — ingredients, benefits, serving, lifestyle
  • Subscribe & save selected by default
  • Remove the quantity selector — she can add more from the cart
  • Show the pack she actually picked. Six-pack? Show six.
  • Banner + free shipping above the fold
  • Collapse the padding
Ninety of a hundred people left on this page. These seven fixes cost nothing and no app is required.
Which is worth saying to a merchant who is about to buy their fourth app: the biggest leak on your site is free to fix.
07
8% add to cart, 1% conversion — something is broken
Ninety percent of the time it is just shipping.
$30 product. $20 to ship.
She added it, saw the total, and left. It is not the price of the product — it is the surprise.
And when it is not shipping
  • a button covering the button
  • cart page and drawer cart both loading
  • a loyalty widget over the checkout button
  • a support widget over the CTA
  • three payment options instead of fifteen
  • targeting a country they cannot ship to
One of those is our app.

“A loyalty button that is 10% off if you claim it — but the checkout button is here, and a support button there, and the only spot you can click is this little spot.”
— and that is a conversion killer.
Our widget can be the reason

Which is why placement is not a preference. It costs the merchant money.
So when a merchant says
“can you move the widget?”
That is not fussiness. They may have watched their conversion rate fall.
Move it — then ask what changed.
The one app on their site you are responsible for is on the shortlist of things that break checkout.
Nobody else in this company is going to tell them. You will be the one who sees it.
09
Decoder row · low AOV
Not enough money per order.
The fixes, fastest first
  • Multi-packs — a 4-pack or 6-pack at a discount instead of one unit
  • Pre-built pairs — moisturizer + exfoliator, chosen for them
  • Variation packs — three colours of the lip gloss in one box
  • Upsell in cart, on site, and in the popup
  • Cross-sell into things that pair
The threshold rule
If their average order is $39, set free shipping at $40.
She has to spend one dollar more — so she adds a $15 item.
And gift thresholds
Spend $100, get a free hat. Spend $150, get something better.
A floor worth knowing: below about $35 an order, almost nothing scales.
The ad cost and the shipping eat it. When a merchant with a $22 average order asks why ads are not working — that is usually the answer, and it is not an ads problem.

11
Decoder row · low conversion, high AOV
This one is normal.
Do not panic. Do not escalate.
The example to remember
A $1,700 portable basketball shooting machine.
It is never going to convert like a $30 candle. Nobody buys one on a Tuesday because they saw a reel.
And it still works
Cheap reach, plenty of clicks, few buyers — but each one is worth so much that the maths is fine on their costs.
Payment plans nudge it up a little. That is all it needs.
Knowing which numbers are supposed to look bad is part of the job.
A merchant panicking about a 0.4% conversion rate on a $1,700 product needs reassurance, not a fix — and being the person who can say that calmly is worth a lot.
12
A trap you will meet in a real account
Sales are flat.
But ROAS looks great.
What is actually happening
The ads are being shown to people who already buy. They would have bought anyway. Meta counts the sale, ROAS looks wonderful, and the business does not grow a dollar.
The number that reveals it
80%
returning customers
Healthy on its own. Fatal if it is also who the ads are hitting.
A high returning rate is a good thing. It becomes a problem only when acquisition stops reaching anyone new.
The fix is not our product — it is excluding past customers and the email list from acquisition. But spotting it is exactly the AM read, and almost nobody spots it.
13
Low returning rate — the fix depends entirely on what they sell
Consumable
soap, supplements, skincare
Subscription is the biggest lever, then reminders and remarketing. This is where loyalty works hardest.
Clothing & repeat-catalogue
Do not just push one hero SKU. Show past buyers the 2nd to 5th best sellers, new arrivals, the next drop. Loyalty and credit help here, but range does more.
High-ticket, bought once
Sell them a consumable accessory — the cleaning kit, the refill, the spare. That is how a one-time product gets a second order at all.
So “they do not come back” is three different problems with three different answers.
And only one of them is mainly ours.

19
One job, taken to ten out of ten
Alia
field: capture — a popup
Everyone ships a popup. Alia’s whole company rests on noticing what a normal one does.
“Blanket discount popups buy signups that never convert — and train shoppers to wait for 15% off.”
Their words, not ours.
That is fear #2, and the deals trap, in one sentence.
Alia did not build a better popup. They noticed the popup was causing the merchant’s problem — so they made the coupon earned instead of given. Then went deep, not wide: twelve researched formats, AI triggering, testing. One job, done to ten.

An app that exists because of one ticket
Order
Editing
one job: let her fix an unfulfilled order
“A wrong address, size, variant or forgotten item becomes a support ticket — and can turn into a mis-shipment if the warehouse acts first.”
You have all worked this ticket. Somebody built a company around it.
She mistypes her address30 seconds of her time
She emails supporta ticket in your queue
The warehouse ships firsta parcel in the wrong place
Refund or reshipthe order’s whole margin, gone
She tells someoneand leaves a review
A ticket is never just a ticket. It has a cost downstream — and that cost is why the app exists.
See the chain and you stop clearing tickets and start reading them as evidence about the business. That is the whole difference between the two jobs.
21
Finding problems is the easy half
Which one do you fix first?
Rank by two things only
1 · How many people does it lose?
90 of 100 left at the product page. Nothing else on the site is close.
2 · What does it cost to fix?
Three bullets and better photos cost nothing. A subscription programme costs months.
So the order is usually
1. something is broken — free to fix, fix today
2. the product page — free, biggest leak
3. shipping and checkout — cheap, second biggest
4. AOV — bundles, thresholds
5. coming back — slowest, and where we live
Notice where we come.
Last. A merchant with a broken checkout button does not need a loyalty program — and if we sell them one, it will not work, and they will be right to blame us. Recommending someone else’s fix first is how you earn the conversation about ours.
