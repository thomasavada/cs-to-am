# Live talk — problem + heading

**Length:** 20–25 min, then sit down.
**Audience:** Joy CS.
**Job of this talk:** show them the two jobs, name what they do today, name where they must head.
**Not this talk:** Joy settings, earning rules, Chase, English class, the four-rep dump.

One line they should repeat:

> From: app support. To: retention service — get the shop more coming back, in less time.

---

## Arc

```
1. Two jobs        ← the compare + why now (AI). the hook.
2. What you do today ← the problem
3. Why you freeze    ← the diagnosis
4. Why this job exists
5. Where you are heading
6. How we will learn
7. Close
```

**Delivery note — do not flip 1 and 2.** The compare first is aspirational: *there is a bigger job.*
The problem then lands as *here is what is between you and it.* Reversed, you open by telling the room
they are bad at their job, and they defend for the next twenty minutes.

---

## 1. Two jobs (6 min) — open here

**Do not open with a table. Open with a ticket.**

Slide: one line, nothing else.

> **"Rewards aren't working."**

Ask the room out loud: *what do you write back?*
Take two or three answers. Someone will say **"could you explain more."** Let that sit. Do not correct it yet.

Then reveal:

| What we write today | What the other job writes |
|---|---|
| "Could you please provide more details?" | "Members aren't using the reward, so it isn't creating a second order." |
| → merchant explains again | → "Do they **not see** the balance, or they see it and **it's not worth** using?" |
| → copy → forward | → merchant answers one question, once |

Both take the same number of minutes. **Only one of them did the thinking.**

Then the table — five rows, no more:

| | **CS** | **AM** |
|---|---|---|
| You are judged on | the **ticket** | the **shop** |
| Who starts | they write to you | **you** open it |
| You must know | the **app** | the **business** |
| Done means | ticket closed | the **result showed up** |
| You lose when | backlog, slow reply | churn you never saw coming |

Land it:

> You can close every ticket perfectly and still lose the account.

That is not a complaint about CS. It is the reason the second job exists.

### And now the part that changed

Slide — three lines, nothing else:

```
"Where do I turn on points?"      →  a machine answers this. instantly. 24/7. perfect English.
"Should they run points at all?"  →  a machine cannot own this.
"Did their customers come back?"  →  a machine cannot own this.
```

Look at the table again. The left column is **execution**. The right column is **outcome**.

> AI does not take the job. It takes the **execution**.
> The outcome is what is left for people. That is why you move — not someday, now.

Execution is the part that is being automated, by us, at Joy, this year. Nobody has automated
being **accountable** for whether it worked.

Seed the next beats with this — do not explain it yet, just say it:

> Notice which half the machine is best at: **the English.** Writing a clear, polite, correct reply
> is the easiest thing it does. If your plan is to win on English, you picked the one race that is
> already lost. What the machine does not have is a picture of **this merchant's shop.**

---

## 2. What you do today (4 min) — the problem

Show **3 real tickets** (anonymized). Two columns:

| You answered | They meant |
|---|---|
| How to turn points on | "Will people come back if I do this?" |
| Where the VIP page is | "Is this worth running, or am I wasting the quarter?" |
| Please explain more | I don't have a shop in my head, so I make them do my job |

Say the pattern without shame:

> Merchant talks → I don't get it → "can you explain more?" → they write the ticket for me → I copy → I forward.

That is transcription. That is **answering the app**. The app question is the wrong job.

---

## 3. Why you freeze (3 min) — the diagnosis

Not English.

Without a picture of how a **real shop** earns — get a customer, take money, need them back — their words
have nowhere to land. So every sentence is noise. So you ask them to speak until you can forward it.

Call back to the opening:

> The machine already has perfect English. It still cannot answer this merchant.
> So the missing thing was never English.

Say this in the room (VN is fine):

> Không phải vì tiếng Anh. Vì chưa biết shop chạy thế nào, nên câu của merchant không ghim vào đâu được.
> Học xong, em phải nói lại được vấn đề. Hỏi "explain more" là chưa hiểu — không phải là cách hiểu.

---

## 4. Why this job exists at all (3 min)

Now they will accept it, because they have seen the gap.

We are SaaS. **Service.** Expertise on ecom and retention.

> If we sold an app, there would only ever be support.
> We sell a **result**, so somebody has to own whether it shows up.

And now that machines do the execution, owning the result is not the senior version of the job.
It **is** the job.

The merchant is a **business**. The only question in their head:

> If I launch a loyalty program, do I get something back? Do more customers come back? Do I **earn more**?

They will spend. They are not shopping to spend less. They want an outcome, in **less time** —
setup speed is part of the service.

| They are not | They are |
|---|---|
| Users of an App Store app | Operators of a shop |
| Paying for buttons and settings | Paying for **customers coming back** |
| Asking "how does this app work?" | Asking "will this make me money?" |
| Someone to train on a UI | Someone to get to **outcome in less time** |

---

## 5. Where you are heading (3 min)

There is a step in between. Nobody jumps.

```
CS                answers the app
  ↓   gate: restate the merchant's outcome before you reply
CS who reads      answers the ticket behind the ticket
  ↓   gate: read a brand cold. launch your own store.
AM                answers for the account
```

The middle rung is a real job and a real raise. Aim there first.

After this path you can answer four things about **any** merchant, **before** Joy:

1. What does this shop sell?
2. Why would a person buy a **second** time?
3. What do they expect if they launch loyalty — **more coming back / more earned**?
4. How do we get them to that result **faster**?

If you cannot answer 1–3, you are not allowed to open the app. The app is the last mile, not the job.

---

## 6. How we will learn that (4 min)

**Not theory. Not memorising.** You remember nothing you did not use.

**We pick a real brand and break it down.** Same sheet, again and again:
what they sell · how they make money on one order · how people arrive · where people quit ·
why anyone buys twice · what apps they run · is it ours.

Four reps. Then you can do it cold in fifteen minutes on a brand you have never seen.

**And you launch a real store — not a development store.**

| Development store | Real (launched) store |
|---|---|
| You are a user clicking Admin | You are the **owner** |
| Nothing is at stake | A wrong discount actually costs you |
| "Set up the app" feels like the work | **First order / second order** is the work |
| You still think like CS | You start thinking like the person on the call |

One product. Real checkout. **Max three apps** — every app has to survive *"what job does this do?"*
Today our stores are many apps, low quality, not to standard. That is the thing being fixed.

Complete **order 1 → attempt order 2.** That is the simulation.

Still **zero Joy** until you can restate the outcome. See [2-course.md](2-course.md).

---

## 7. Close (1 min)

> We don't get paid because they learned the app. They get paid when the same customer orders again.
> Your job is that outcome, faster.

Sit down.
